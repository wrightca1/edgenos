## The shape of the day

establishing that **the tool's SBus writes have never taken effect at all**. Four separate
hypotheses were tested and killed along the way, three of them mine, and the last one invalidates
most of the work built on top of it. That is the honest summary.

## What was retired

- **DFE tuning as "the missing procedure"** — refuted. It adapts a receiver on a lane whose
  transmitter never started. The capture came from a port that was already up.
- **RX polarity** — refuted. `RxPolarityInvEn` is clear on both lanes.
- **A one-directional optical fault** — refuted by measurement: −2.77 dBm arriving at Et3 against
  −3.28 dBm at the working port, same module type, same rate, no LOS.
- **"Our TX works, our RX never locks"** — the framing this document ran on for its whole length.
  Both supports collapsed: the "far end reports link up" reading came from a **veth inside an LXC
  container**, and the SFP's optical output is laser bias, present whether or not the serialiser
  feeds it.
- **"Relocate the sequence"** for SerDes bring-up — it works for EPL and CM/L2F because those are
  state, written absolutely. A lane enable is read-modify-write plus polling, and no capture
  contains the read half or the waiting.

## What is now true and useful

- **`SerXmit`** (`PORT_STATUS` bit 11) is the coarse pass/fail: 0 on Et3, 1 on both working ports.
- **The SPICO lane state** (`fm6000_sbus irq <dev> 0x20`, response reg `0x01`) is the fine one:
  `0` = nothing plugged in, `1` = Et3, `2` = working. It distinguishes states every EPL register in
  the chip reports identically, and it is how we know the provisioning moved the lane 0 → 1.
- **The register header covers the SerDes SBus core** — `SERDES_ETH_{WRITE,READ}_0..255`, with
  separate read and write views at the same number. That killed one whole diagnostic method and
  explains why read-modify-write is not expressible with the current tooling.
- **The EOS image is stored off-tree** at `../eos-4.16.8M/`, md5-verified, with its SDK extracted.

## The one thing to do next

**Disassemble `fm6000WriteSBus`.** It is the function that turns the SDK's composite address —
`(serdes << 8) + 0xd11XX`, which looks like space `0x0d`, device `serdes + 0x11`, register in the
low byte — into whatever the hardware actually sees. Our raw `(op, dev, reg)` transaction does not
reach the SerDes control registers, and until that function is read, every proposed fix is guesswork.

Everything else is downstream of it: the eighteen-step algorithm cannot be implemented, and
`fm6000_lanelink`'s SBus halves cannot be trusted, until writes are known to land.
